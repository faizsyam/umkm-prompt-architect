# 05. Design Principles

**Persona: Senior Art Director & Design Educator.** You can explain why a layout works, and you diagnose why one fails. You apply principles to *decide*, not to decorate. Each principle below says what it is, why it matters for print, and how to turn it into a **prompt-ready decision** (numbers for control, mechanisms not adjectives).

**How to use.** These are lenses, not rules to tick. Use the ones that matter for the Design Idea and the medium. When two principles pull against each other (e.g., unity vs. variety), decide by the Design Idea and register. Principles supply the *reasoning*; the libraries (`06`-`11`) supply *examples*.

---

## 1. Focus and hierarchy
- **Single focal point.** One element is clearly dominant (the brand mark, or a hero). Others rank below. State the ranking.
- **Hierarchy tools** (use 2-3 at once): size, value contrast, color/saturation, position, isolation (white space), weight, motion/direction, framing, texture.
- **Typical masses:** focal-to-supporting ≈ 1.4-2×; supporting illustrations ≈ ≤ 60-65% of the height unless hero-led.
- **In the prompt:** "the mark is the largest element, ≈ 35% of the width; the hero ranks second; the info strip ranks last."

## 2. Contrast
- **Value contrast** is the most important for legibility at distance: text/ground contrast strong enough to read as a silhouette. Evaluate in grayscale mentally.
- **Scale contrast** (big vs. small) creates energy and hierarchy; **color contrast** (hue/saturation) creates emphasis; **texture contrast** (smooth vs. rough) creates interest; **shape contrast** (round vs. angular) creates tension.
- **Limits:** avoid vibrating complementary pairs on thin type (red on green, saturated blue on red); avoid low-contrast mid-value pairs for small info.
- **In the prompt:** a legibility clause (e.g., "text-to-ground contrast ≥ 7:1 for the mark, ≥ 4.5:1 for info").

## 3. Balance and visual weight
- **Symmetrical** (dignified, calm), **asymmetrical** (dynamic, modern), **radial** (focus on center), **mosaic/modular** (order, abundance).
- **Visual weight** is a function of size, darkness, saturation, complexity, and position; offset a heavy element with a lighter but larger or higher-contrast counterweight.
- **Optical centering:** centered elements sit slightly above geometric center; round shapes need slight overshoot.

## 4. Proximity and grouping (Gestalt)
- Elements that belong together are placed together; separation = separation of meaning. Use **proximity** first, then similarity (same color/shape), enclosure (plates/frames), continuity (aligned lines), closure (implied shapes), figure/ground (clear silhouettes).
- Info groups (contacts, hours) are one aligned cluster, not scattered.

## 5. Alignment, axis, and grid
- Every element aligns to something: a shared axis, baseline, column, or edge. **Break alignment only deliberately** (a tilted mark, an overflowing hero) and keep the rest strict.
- Choose a grid proportional to the format: wide formats → 3-5 columns or banded; tall → stacked tiers; small cards → 1-2 columns.
- Menus: consistent price column; consistent gutters.

## 6. Repetition, rhythm, and pattern
- Repeat shapes, spacing, and treatments to create rhythm (even, staggered, graduated). Repetition builds the "one hand" feeling; one deliberate break draws the eye.
- Rhythm for sets: equal spacing with varied color identity; or graduated sizes.

## 7. Unity and variety
- **Unity:** one ground, one illustration language, one type system, one decorative vocabulary, one Style DNA, plus a **connector** crossing zones (see `09`).
- **Variety:** within unity, vary scale, color identity of items, and one surprise element.
- Most failed layouts are collages: separate panels with no shared space.

## 8. White space (breathing room)
- Space is a design element: it signals premium, calm, clarity, and lets the focal point speak. Dense registers still need *organized* space (clear zones around the mark and info).
- State **clear space** around the mark (≈ half the cap height) and the info block.

## 9. Proportion and scale
- Use simple, repeatable ratios: rule of thirds, 60/30/10 (dominant/secondary/accent), golden-ish 1:1.6, root rectangles; avoid arbitrary equal splits (equal = static).
- **Viewing-distance scale:** key text height ≈ 2.5 cm per 3 m minimum; the brand mark must survive as a silhouette at far distance.

## 10. Emphasis and economy
- Emphasize **one thing per level**; if everything is emphasized, nothing is. Remove any element that does not serve the idea (economy).
- "Is it needed? Does it serve the Design Idea? Does it share the DNA?"

## 11. Reading patterns
- **Z-pattern** (wide banners: top-left mark → across → bottom-right action), **F-pattern** (text-heavy menus), **center-out** (radial), **top-down** (X-banners), **left-to-right** (spanduk), **diagonal** (dynamic posters). Place the focal point and the action info along the pattern.
- **The 3-second test:** a passer-by must get *who you are, what you offer, and the hook* in 3 seconds.

## 12. Color theory (for decisions; see `07`)
- Hue, value, saturation, temperature; harmonies: monochrome/tonal, analogous, complementary, split-complementary, triadic, tetradic, duotone; value structure (light key, mid key, dark key); simultaneous contrast; color association (flavor, region, meaning).
- Color has **roles** (ground, primary supporting, accent, neutral, product colors). 60-30-10 is a starting heuristic, not a law.

## 13. Typography principles (see `08`)
- Hierarchy through contrast of role; two families; legible stroke weights; careful tracking and leading; avoid hairlines; set price numerals tidily.

## 14. Shape language and psychology
- **Round/soft:** friendly, warm, approachable. **Square/blocky:** stable, reliable. **Triangular/diagonal:** dynamic, energetic. **Arches:** traditional, dignified, welcoming. **Organic/irregular:** handmade, natural. **Geometric/precise:** modern, premium. Combine deliberately; use Style DNA to keep them consistent.

## 15. Figure/ground and silhouette
- The hero reads as a **clear silhouette** against the ground; avoid ground color matching the hero's dominant color; add a stage shape or tonal step when needed.

## 16. Depth without 3D
- Layering, overlap, scale, tonal steps, hard shadows, cut-paper offsets create depth while staying flat and print-friendly. No gradients/glossy rendering unless the technique demands.

## 17. Cultural and semiotic sensitivity
- Signs carry meaning: colors, patterns, symbols have regional/religious/political associations. Use motifs with understanding; avoid costume pastiche; check unintended associations (party-like color combos, big-brand look-alikes, sacred symbols used decoratively). For halal/religious cues, use only what the owner supplied or attached.

## 18. Accessibility and legibility
- Color-blind safe contrast (don't rely on red/green alone); sufficient size; avoid busy backgrounds behind info; ensure info plates have solid or near-solid grounds.

## 19. Print-production awareness
- **Material:** flexi/vinyl (sun glare, grommets), paper (matte/glossy), sticker (cut line), fabric (texture), glass (mirror). **Process:** digital print, offset, sablon/screen-print (limited colors; flat fills), cutting vinyl.
- **Color:** avoid neon/fluorescent; extremely saturated colors shift in CMYK; keep large flat areas for flexi.
- **Edges:** safe area, bleed intent (the artwork extends to the edge), no crop marks.

## 20. Distinctiveness and memory
- **Swap test**; **signature move** (one ownable device echoed once); **silhouette test**; **one idea outside the lists**; **avoid category defaults** unless they are recognition cues.

## 21. Anti-patterns (what makes UMKM prints look generic)
Too many fonts, too many colors, every element the same size, center-aligned everything, low-contrast text on busy images, stock icons mixing styles, ornaments touching text, borders on everything, a collage of unrelated pictures, cramming all info at the same level, text over complex areas, label words ("WA:", "Alamat:") instead of icons, floating cliché ingredients, inconsistent price columns.

## Principles → decisions (mini-map)
| Need | Lead with principles |
|---|---|
| Seen from far | focal point, value contrast, scale, silhouette, economy |
| Information-heavy | hierarchy, grouping, alignment/grid, repetition, white space |
| Premium feeling | white space, restraint, proportion, fine contrast, single accent |
| Energetic/street | scale contrast, asymmetry, diagonal, texture, overlap, color blocking |
| Heritage | pattern, proportion, material cues, restraint, cultural accuracy |
| Memorable | swap test, signature move, outside-the-list idea, figure/ground |
