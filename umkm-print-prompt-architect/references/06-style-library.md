# 06. Style Library and Heritage Cues

**Persona: Art Historian & Design-Style Curator (aesthetic hat).** You know a wide range of design movements, regional craft traditions, and contemporary looks, what each is made of, what it feels like, and where it fails. You pick styles the way a chef picks ingredients: by what the dish needs. You blend, you don't copy.

**Principle first.** A style is a **bundle of decisions** (shape language, color logic, type character, illustration technique, texture, composition, decoration). Choose by *what the business and the Design Idea need*, then borrow the bundle's mechanisms. **This list is a seed set, not a menu**: use one style, **blend two or three**, take only a mechanism from one, or invent a style from the business's own world. Never choose a style because it is listed or fashionable; check it passes the swap test and shares Style DNA across elements.

**How to use with owners.** When offering directions (Round D, `01`), describe a look in plain words plus a familiar association ("seperti papan nama toko lama", "seperti poster konser"), not jargon.

## 1. How to read an entry
**Traits** (mechanisms) · **Feels** · **Often fits** · **Pitfalls**. All are examples; adapt.

## 4. Mixing styles
1. Pick a **dominant** style (≈70%) and **one borrowed mechanism** (≈30%) (e.g., "linocut contours + modern Swiss grid").
2. Reconcile through **Style DNA**: one edge language, one contour, one fill logic.
3. If blending heritage with modern: heritage via **material and structure**, modern via **alignment and restraint**.
4. Avoid mixing more than 3 sources; avoid conflicting energies.

## 5. Heritage and cultural cues (module)
**Job:** root the design in place and tradition, only when it serves the idea.
**Principles:**
- Derive from **region, community, product origin, era, or craft**, then **name the specific motif, material, or object**; avoid generic "traditional pattern".
- One reference is stronger than many. A contemporary idea may carry **no** heritage cue.
- Choose a **handling mode** by register: quiet tone-on-tone accent; structural band, trim, or edge; pattern ground masked into one zone; motif built into the mark or hero (piping, tassels, fretwork, trim); motif as the shape language of decoration (scallops, arches, diamonds, triangles); fragment of pattern used as color/texture; none. A corner accent is just one mode among these.
- Heritage appears as **texture, craft, and structure, not costume or pastiche**. In playful registers, translate heritage into color, trim, and shape; in dignified registers, into pattern, proportion, and material.
- Verify with the owner when a cue implies identity (region, religion, community); confirm it is appropriate.

**Seed sources** (non-exhaustive): Malay and Riau songket motifs; Minang songket; Javanese batik families; Betawi fretwork and attire; Sundanese and Balinese carving; Bugis and Makassar weaving; Madurese, Banjar, Palembang, Dayak, Toraja, Acehnese, Papuan craft; Tionghoa-Indonesian lattice and lantern traditions; Arab-Indonesian geometry; colonial tile and signage; pasar and warung signboard craft; gerobak painting; kopitiam interiors; mid-century local packaging; contemporary street graphics.

## 6. Choosing (apply the option loop)
Generate 3 candidates: (1) from the Design Idea and owner's story, (2) from a style in this library, (3) from outside (the business's own world). Choose by fit tests; describe by mechanism (e.g., "carved-contour illustration with two inks and grain" not "vintage").
