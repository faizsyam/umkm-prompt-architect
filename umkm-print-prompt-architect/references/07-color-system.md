# 07. Color System

**Persona: Colorist & Print Color Specialist.** You choose color for meaning, hierarchy, and legibility at distance, and you know how color behaves on flexi under the sun, on paper, and next to the neighbor's sign. You choose a strategy first, then roles, then hues.

**Principle first.** Color does four jobs: **carry mood, create hierarchy, guarantee legibility, honor the brand.** Choose a **strategy**, assign **roles**, derive hues from **meaning**, cap the palette, and add a legibility clause. The strategy and hue lists are **seeds, not menus**: mix, adapt, or invent.

---

## 1. Principles
- Choose a **strategy** first, then **roles**: Ground (~55-65%), Primary supporting (~20-30%: mark, type, linework), Accents (1-2, ~5-12%), Neutrals (<5%). Food/product colors are confined to the illustrations.
- Choose the **ground by purpose**: viewing distance, setting, mood, brand color, and contrast with the hero. Light, mid, saturated, and dark grounds are all legitimate; decide, don't default.
- Choose hues from **meaning**: flavor and ingredient colors, region and craft, community conventions, the brand's own color. Saturation follows energy (calm = muted; spirited = confident but controlled; never neon or fluorescent).
- **Given brand colors** are kept in hue identity; adjust value, saturation, and proportion until coherent and legible. If a **logo is attached**, extract its colors; the palette must harmonize with the logo and keep it legible on the ground (plate if necessary).
- Cap the palette (e.g. ≤ 4 hues plus neutrals) and state "no other hues", plus a legibility clause (text-to-ground contrast strong enough for distance; avoid vibrating pairs on thin type).
- Test in **grayscale**: the hierarchy must survive without hue.
- Check **environment and look-alike risk** (`02` §E): do the colors blend into the neighbors, or copy a big brand?

## 2. Strategy seeds (examples, not a closed set)
tonal light ground, or tonal mid-value ground, or tonal dark ground · bold color-field ground with contrasting mark plaque · dark ground with luminous accents, or jewel-tone ground · duotone, or tritone, or limited-ink print palette · complementary pair, or split-complementary · analogous warm family, or analogous cool family · triadic with one dominant · neutral ground with one signature accent · color-blocked bands or panels · monochrome with a single hot accent · paper-and-ink (cream + one or two inks).

## 3. Harmonies in plain words
| Harmony | Result | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Monochrome/tonal | calm, elegant | premium, calm |
| Analogous | harmonious, warm or cool | fresh, cozy |
| Complementary | vivid, punchy (control proportion) | street, promo |
| Split-complementary | punch with less tension | balanced energy |
| Triadic | playful variety (keep one dominant) | kids, fun |
| Duotone/limited ink | graphic, cost-aware, crafty | riso/screen-print look |
| Neutral + accent | confident, modern | modern-minimal |

## 4. Meaning and association (cues to verify with the owner; locally contextual)
- **Warm reds/oranges/yellows:** appetite, energy, warmth, celebration; also commonly used by chain fast food (look-alike risk). Red also carries luck/prosperity in Tionghoa-Indonesian contexts.
- **Greens:** freshness, health, nature; also associated with Islamic tradition and some big chains.
- **Blues/teals:** trust, cleanliness, water, calm; typical for laundry/clinic (very common; may need a twist).
- **Browns/earth/cream:** warmth, craft, coffee/baked goods, tradition.
- **Black/charcoal:** premium, bold, night; **white/cream:** clean, space; **gold/brass/ochre:** richness, heritage (use muted metallic-looking flat ochre; no glossy gradients).
- **Pinks/lilac/pastels:** sweet, soft, youth (can read childish if unbalanced).
- **Purples:** creativity, luxury; sometimes political or religious associations; check context.
- Local conventions: ingredient colors (chili red, turmeric yellow, pandan green, gula merah brown, ube/taro purple), regional cloths (batik soga browns, indigo), festival colors. Use specifically.

## 5. Ground decision
- **Light ground:** clean, appetizing, great for menus; use for close reading.
- **Mid-value ground:** relaxed, can be neutral for both light and dark elements.
- **Saturated ground:** strong from far; needs a calm mark plaque or careful contrast.
- **Dark ground:** premium, night, luminous food; check legibility of small text.
- **Textured ground:** adds craft; keep behind text subtle.

## 6. Print behavior
- Avoid neon/fluorescent; very saturated blue-violets and cyans shift in CMYK; extremely dark flat blacks band on flexi (use rich dark tones intentionally); thin light-on-dark text bleeds (thicken it); outdoor sun reduces perceived contrast (push value difference).
- For sablon/screen-print or sticker: **≤ 4 flat colors**, no gradients, hard edges.

## 7. Writing the palette in the prompt
- Strategy + ground decision.
- Roles with usage levels and (optional) hex ranges: "Ground: warm cream (#F2E6CF-#EAD9B8, ≈60%); Primary: deep indigo (#1F2A5A, ≈25%); Accent: ochre (#C98A1B, ≈8%)".
- Food/product colors confined to illustrations.
- "No other hues."
- Legibility clause.
- If a logo is attached: "Palette harmonizes with the attached logo's colors; the logo remains unchanged."
